The two critical errors (both fixed)

nonFiniteVerbsOverviewAn exercise asked what "swimming" is in the swimming pool and keyed present participle. It's a gerund (a pool for swimming) — the item's own hint gave the gerund test while labelling it a participle. Replaced with a genuine participle item (the barking dog).
reflexivePronounsEmphasisThe "common mistake" paragraph illustrated the emphatic reflexive with "I taught myself Spanish" — which is actually a true reflexive (the paragraph's own gloss admitted it), teaching the misclassification it warned against. The dominant pattern: two defensible answers

The engine is multiple-choice with a single exact-match key and no accept-list. So any item where a distractor is also correct in some register or variety is unwinnable and cannot be patched in data — it must be rewritten. ~64 such items were confirmed and rewritten, including:

  • Genuinely interchangeable forms: each vs all ("one by one" doesn't exclude "all"); could vs was able to for general past ability; many vs a lot of in affirmatives; opinion-vs-size adjective order ("big ugly" ≈ "ugly big"); optional backshift in reported speech; mandative subjunctive vs British finite ("suggested that we went").
  • AmE/BrE variety keys — the notable cluster for a global-audience site: at vs on the weekend; present-perfect keys with American "just ate"/"already finished" among the distractors. The site's own britishVsAmerican.ts documents those American forms as correct, so it was penalising exactly what another page teaches. Made variety-neutral rather than picking a side — the items now work in every variety.
  • 5 single-pattern drills (get-passive, had-better, past modals, reflexives, shall/will) where all 10–20 items tested one mechanical decision — diversified so they actually teach.

What verification caught

The findings held; the fixes needed rework. Three refute-first verifiers confirmed almost every finding (only 2 of ~70 dropped: one mis-cited reason-clause item, one "go in the room" overstatement softened rather than rewritten). But they rejected or rebuilt a large share of the proposed fixes — every one of the 5 single-pattern-drill replacements, and several two-defensible rewrites, would have reintroduced the very ambiguity they were removing (e.g. a replacement using "much progress", which is standard formal English; a get-passive item where "got cracked" ≈ "cracked"). On this site the verification layer functioned less as a false-positive filter and more as a fix-hardening pass — which is exactly why no replacement shipped on trust.

English explanations — 136 topics, remarkably clean

Beyond the one reflexive critical: the "its" standalone-possessive list error (no such form exists), and ~6 minors (a broken example that didn't parse, an intonation description with the fall on the wrong word, a "raised is irregular" slip, an unmarked British "in hospital"). Notably, reviewers specifically hunted the high-damage class of bogus L1-interference causal claims — and found none. The author's causal explanations (pro-drop, zero-article, 3rd-person -s) are all sound. The reference datasets (150 irregular verbs, 69 false friends across 5 languages, punctuation, linking words) were clean but for one garbled gloss.

Reported, not fixed (product decisions)

  • English-only practice surface. Explanations are 9-language, but exercises and their post-answer feedback are English-only — so a learner reading in Arabic or Chinese hits an all-English practice layer. Translating 2,810 questions × 8 languages (~22k strings) is a project in its own right, not an audit fix.
  • A structural safeguard worth adding: the engine's lack of an accept-list is the root cause of the whole two-defensible-answers class. An optional accept: string[] field on the question type would let future ambiguous items be handled in data rather than by rewriting.
  • A few near-duplicate questions within topics, and three oversized "mistake" callouts, left as optional polish.

Method & verification

9 parallel reviewers (exercises exhaustive across 6 batches; English explanations across 3, the C1/C2 batch flagged for extra rigour) → all findings written to a manifest file in the repo before any change (resilience — this run survived several session-limit interruptions with zero lost work) → 3 refute-first verifiers, each also validating that every replacement was itself free of the two-defensible-answers defect → repairs → the repo's own gates (validate · readability · lint · build, all green; the readability ceiling caught one over-long rewrite, which was split) → the 8 non-English languages reconciled to the 7 meaning-changing English fixes, index-aligned so the paragraph-count invariant held. CEFR levels were left untouched throughout — they gate a dormant paywall, so any level change was flag-only.
