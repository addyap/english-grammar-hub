What was wrong — and the single pattern behind it

Almost every defect is one thing: a stem that doesn't pin down the tense/word it's testing, so a distractor is also a correct English sentence. The classic case (the one that started this audit) — "He ___ (not/listen) to music." — accepts both the continuous isn't listening and the simple doesn't listen because nothing says when. The fix is always to add the missing cue, matched to the exercise's own teaching point.

The heaviest cluster was the tense-contrast drills (contrastExercises.ts, 11 of 20): these deliberately pit two confusable tenses against each other, and repeatedly offered a past-simple option that is independently grammatical — e.g. "We finished the entire project" or "…have worked since January" are both perfectly correct, not traps.

All 136 topic files plus contrastExercises.ts were audited item-by-item — no sampling. The defect rate was highest, as expected, in tense and tense-contrast topics, and effectively zero in structural topics (articles, quantifiers, word order, agreement, suffixes, prepositions of place).

Method

  • Scope: one defect class — items where ≥2 options are genuinely correct, plus any wrong/missing answer key. Explanation wording, translation nuance and level-tuning were out of scope for this pass.
  • Coverage: 7 parallel reviewers split the 136 files + contrast set into chunks; every question was evaluated (one chunk was re-run after a session-limit interruption).
  • Precision bias: reviewers were told to flag only defensibly-correct alternates and to log anything doubtful as "borderline" rather than assert it — protecting against false positives.
  • Verification: every confirmed finding and its proposed rewrite was re-checked (register, British/American variation, and whether the fix reintroduces the ambiguity) before editing.
  • Repair: because the engine matches one exact answer, each item was rewritten so exactly one option is correct — the stem's new cue reinforces the exercise's teaching point. A resumable manifest was written first.
  • Gates: validate (structure + all languages in sync), readability (A1/A2 ≤45w, B1+ ≤70w), and full build — all pass.

Follow-up: borderline items tightened 15 more fixed

After the main pass, the borderline items were revisited and the genuinely-ambiguous ones fixed too:

  • American-English present perfect (9): presentPerfect.ts ×5 and contrastExercises.ts ×4 no longer mark the AmE past-simple wrong — the AmE-valid distractor was swapped for a form error, or a "so far" anchor now forces the present perfect in every variety. Variety-neutral.
  • narrativeTenses.ts causal group (4): the past-simple distractor (defensible after because/before) was replaced with present-perfect forms — a real learner error and unambiguously wrong in a past narrative — so only the past-perfect (continuous) answer stands.
  • Never/Seldom inversion (2): "did I see" is also a valid inversion, so a present-relevance anchor ("…as the one tonight") now forces the present perfect.

Deliberately left as-is (not bugs — each lesson's own explicit teaching point): the likeVsAs "as vs like + clause" items (the lesson labels like the standard-English error) and bothEitherNeither's "Neither of the answers is correct" (its explanation already frames the singular as "careful English"). Making the informal option ungrammatical there would delete the rule the lesson teaches.
